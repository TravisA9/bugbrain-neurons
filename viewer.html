Growth viewer: what you see

  • The brain is grown in a sheet of tissue from one genome: the cells divide, migrate, choose a fate, become neurons, and send out axons. The axons follow chemical gradients and make synapses where address tags match. Afterwards, neurons without useful output die and spontaneous activity refines the wiring. See How the brain grows.
  • Orientation: front (sensors) is on the left, back (outputs) on the right, and the body midline runs horizontally.
  • Timeline (bottom): 40 development steps, then the stages after growth (selection, trophic competition, 4 juvenile activity blocks, Hebbian pruning). Drag the slider or press Space to play or pause; ← → step through it.
  • Zoom with the mouse wheel, pan by dragging; double-click or F fits the view. Hover for a summary, and click a cell, sensor or output to inspect it. Its synapses are drawn: outgoing in orange, incoming in cyan, inhibitory in red. Its lineage is shown dashed.
  • Layers (right): a morphogen heat map, cells (coloured by tag, fate or population), axons, growth cones, synapses (white = excitatory, red = inhibitory, yellow = plastic), pruned synapses (magenta), deaths (✕ red = programmed, orange = lost trophic competition / afferents) and labels.
  • Wiring diagram (W): populations of the final brain are nodes, with sensors on the left and outputs on the right. Columns show the synaptic distance from the sensors. Edge width = number of synapses; green = excitatory, red = inhibitory, dashed yellow = mostly plastic. Click a node to highlight its links.
  • ▶ Run brain lives a lifetime in the environment and overlays the activity: cells flash when they spike, and population nodes glow with their firing rate.
